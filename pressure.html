Pressure Calibrator

Generate, measure, and calibrate pressure for gauges, transmitters, and switches.

Essential Calibration

🔍 What is a Pressure Calibrator?

A pressure calibrator is a tool used to generate and measure pressure for testing and calibrating pressure instruments. It combines a pressure source (hand pump) and a precision pressure gauge (reference) in one portable package.

  • 📤 Generate Pressure: Use a hand pump (pneumatic or hydraulic) to create pressure
  • 📥 Measure Pressure: Read pressure with a reference-grade sensor
  • 📊 Calibrate: Verify and adjust pressure transmitters, gauges, and switches
  • 🔄 Test: Check pressure switches for setpoint and deadband

🔢 Types of Pressure Calibrators

🔹 Pneumatic (Air/Gas) Calibrators

  • Low Pressure: -1 to 2 bar (-14.5 to 30 psi)
  • Medium Pressure: Up to 20 bar (300 psi)
  • High Pressure: Up to 100 bar (1,500 psi)
  • Common Brands: Fluke 718, Beamex POC, Druck DPI 610

🔹 Hydraulic (Liquid) Calibrators

  • Used for very high pressures (up to 700 bar / 10,000 psi)
  • Uses oil or water as the pressure medium
  • Example: Fluke 7252, Beamex PG series
  • Pros: Generates extreme pressures, very stable
  • Cons: Messy, requires cleaning

📋 How to Calibrate a Pressure Transmitter

Equipment Needed:

  • Pressure calibrator (with hand pump and reference gauge)
  • Loop calibrator or multimeter (to measure 4-20mA output)
  • Appropriate fittings and tubing

Step-by-Step:

  1. Isolate the transmitter from the process
  2. Connect the calibrator to the transmitter's pressure port
  3. Connect the loop calibrator to measure mA output
  4. Apply zero pressure and note mA reading (should be 4mA)
  5. Apply span pressure and note mA reading (should be 20mA)
  6. Check intermediate points: 25%, 50%, 75% of span
  7. Adjust if needed using zero and span adjustments
  8. Record readings on a calibration sheet
✅ Best Practice: Always do a 5-point calibration (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) to verify linearity.
